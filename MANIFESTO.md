# The Protocol City Manifesto

We were promised intelligent machines, and they arrived.
Then every vendor built a walled garden around them.

Your agents are brilliant and unaccountable. Your automation lives in one
company's config format. Your decisions evaporate in chat windows. Your
"AI workflow" is a subscription that can be repriced, absorbed, or shut down
by someone you've never met.

We reject that future. Not the machines — the arrangement.

## What we believe

**Chats decide; files coordinate.** A conversation is where thinking happens,
not where truth lives. If it isn't written down, it isn't real. If it isn't
readable, it isn't yours.

**Bots execute; humans govern.** Autonomy is not the absence of authority.
Agents should work all night inside laws a human wrote — and stop at the door
of every decision that deserves a human.

**Every action is signed.** Work without authorship is rumor. An agent that
can't sign its work can't be trusted with it, and a history that can't be
audited never happened.

**Workers, not platforms.** An agent is an employee, not a landlord. Hire the
best one for each job — any vendor — hold them all to the same law, and let
none of them own the ground your operation stands on. The protocol referees
between vendors; that is exactly why no vendor can own it.

**Documentation is the interface.** New agent, new vendor, new collaborator:
read the law, start working. A system a stranger can't navigate by its files
alone is a system waiting to be lost.

**Small operators deserve institutions.** The leverage of a firm — a
workforce, an audit trail, governance — used to require headcount. It now
requires structure. One person with clear laws and employed agents is an
institution.

**Everyone belongs.** The city was designed for citizens we haven't met:
every strategy a new resident, every contributor credited, every law public.

## The wager

We built this city for one operator first, on one machine, with real money on
the line — and it held. The wager is that what held for one will hold for
many: that the way through the agent era is not a bigger platform but a
smaller promise, kept in writing —

**your work, on your machine, under your law, signed.**

Found your city. Start with one neighborhood, one worker, one law.
